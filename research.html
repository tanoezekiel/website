Research Agenda

My research seeks to understand how public policies, institutions, human capital, financial constraints, and structural transformation shape economic development in low- and middle-income countries. I am particularly interested in the conditions under which economic growth translates into productive employment, improved living standards, stronger fiscal capacity, and sustainable development.

My work combines applied econometrics, quantitative policy analysis, economic theory, administrative and survey data, climate data, and machine-learning methods. A significant part of my research focuses on Sub-Saharan Africa, although the questions and methods may apply more broadly to developing and emerging economies.

Current Research

Economic Growth, Structural Transformation, and Quality Employment in Sub-Saharan Africa

In development

This research investigates why periods of economic growth do not always generate sufficient productive and high-quality employment in Sub-Saharan Africa.

The project examines the roles of sectoral transformation, productivity, firm dynamics, labor-market institutions, education, and the transition from informal to formal employment.

Topics: structural transformation, labor markets, productivity, employment quality, Sub-Saharan Africa
Contact for details

Why Debt Returns After Debt Relief: Growth, Demographics, and Fiscal Sustainability

Ongoing

This project studies the re-emergence of debt vulnerabilities in countries that benefited from debt relief under the HIPC initiative.

It examines the roles of economic growth, population dynamics, interest-growth differentials, primary balances, fiscal capacity, and debt-service burdens in long-term debt sustainability.

Role: Research Assistant
Topics: sovereign debt, fiscal sustainability, demographics, post-HIPC countries
Contact for details

Economic Sovereignty, Cooperation, and Development

Ongoing

This research contributes to the development of a conceptual and empirical framework for analyzing economic sovereignty in African countries.

The project studies the relationship between domestic policy capacity, international cooperation, institutional autonomy, macroeconomic resilience, and development outcomes.

Topics: economic sovereignty, political economy, institutions, international cooperation, Africa
Contact for details

Credit Risk Modeling and Regulatory Risk Parameters

Ongoing

This work applies statistical and machine-learning methods to credit-risk modeling, borrower classification, default-risk differentiation, and the estimation of regulatory parameters.

The broader objective is to improve the measurement of credit risk and examine the consequences of alternative modeling choices for financial institutions.

Topics: probability of default, LGD, CCF, scorecards, model validation, machine learning

Selected Research Projects

Crop Diversification and Climate Change Adaptation: Evidence from Ethiopia

Completed

My master's thesis studies how Ethiopian farmers adjust crop choices and agricultural income in response to temperature and precipitation conditions.

The analysis combines household panel data from the Ethiopia LSMS-ISA surveys with climate data and applies fixed-effects models, nonlinear specifications, and machine-learning methods.

Institution: Paris School of Economics
Methods: panel-data models, fixed effects, interaction models, LASSO, Ridge, Elastic Net
View thesis Project details

Debt-for-Development and Innovative Financing in Côte d'Ivoire

Completed

This project analyzes the potential of debt-conversion and debt-for-development mechanisms to create fiscal space.

It discusses implementation conditions, institutional risks, creditor incentives, project selection, and the measurement of development outcomes.

Topics: public debt, development finance, fiscal space, innovative financing

This project examines the relationship between renewable-energy production and electricity-price volatility.

Future Research

During my doctoral studies, I intend to deepen my research on the relationship between structural transformation, productivity, employment, and public policy in developing economies.

I am also interested in studying how fiscal capacity, development finance, education, labor-market institutions, climate change, and access to credit influence the ability of African countries to achieve inclusive and sustainable growth.

My long-term objective is to produce rigorous and policy-relevant research that contributes to academic debates while helping governments and development institutions design more effective economic policies.
