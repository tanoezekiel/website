Policy Briefs

Debt-for-Development Financing in Côte d'Ivoire

Analysis of debt conversion mechanisms and their potential for creating fiscal space for development investment.

March 2026 Policy Brief Published

Context: Côte d'Ivoire faces significant development financing needs in infrastructure, education, and health. Traditional debt financing has been constrained by fiscal sustainability concerns.

Proposed mechanism: Debt-for-development swaps could redirect debt service payments toward domestic development priorities while reducing the overall debt burden.

Recommendations: The government should explore partnerships with bilateral and multilateral creditors to design debt conversion mechanisms that align with national development priorities.

Renewable Energy and Economic Development in Africa

Policy note on the economic implications of renewable energy investment and the trade-offs with environmental protection.

February 2026 Policy Note Published

Context: African countries are increasingly investing in renewable energy to address energy access gaps and climate change concerns. However, these investments often involve complex trade-offs with environmental protection.

Key issues: This note examines the economic benefits and environmental costs of renewable energy projects, with particular attention to wind farms near protected areas.

Recommendation: Environmental impact assessments should be integrated into the project planning process from the outset, and compensation mechanisms should be established for affected communities.

Research Notes

Debt Reaccumulation After HIPC Debt Relief

Preliminary findings on the re-emergence of debt vulnerabilities in post-HIPC countries across Africa.

July 2026 Working Paper In Progress

Abstract: This paper examines the re-emergence of public debt vulnerabilities in countries that reached the completion point of the Heavily Indebted Poor Countries initiative. Using a panel dataset of post-HIPC countries, we analyze the roles of economic growth, demographic dynamics, fiscal capacity, and debt-service pressures in driving debt reaccumulation.

Key findings: Our results suggest that countries with slower population growth and stronger primary balances are better able to maintain debt sustainability after debt relief.

Climate Adaptation and Crop Diversification in Ethiopia

Extended analysis of the master's thesis with additional robustness checks and heterogeneity analysis.

June 2026 Research Note Draft

Abstract: This research note extends the analysis of my master's thesis on crop diversification and climate change adaptation in Ethiopia. We incorporate additional robustness checks, including alternative climate specifications and heterogeneity analysis by crop type and household characteristics.

Main findings: The results confirm that Ethiopian farmers adjust their crop choices in response to temperature and precipitation changes. However, the magnitude and direction of these responses vary significantly across crops and regions.

Philosophie de Vie

L'Économie au Service de l'Humain

Réflexion sur la finalité de l'économie et sa capacité à servir le bien-être humain au-delà de la simple croissance.

2026 Essai Brouillon

Résumé : Cet essai explore la question fondamentale de la finalité de l'économie. Au-delà de la croissance du PIB, l'économie devrait-elle servir le bien-être humain, la justice sociale et la durabilité environnementale ?

Argument central : L'économie est un outil au service de l'humain, pas une fin en soi. Les politiques économiques devraient être évaluées non seulement sur leur capacité à générer de la croissance, mais aussi sur leur contribution au bien-être des populations.

La Valeur du Savoir dans une Société en Mutation

Essai sur l'importance de la connaissance, de l'éducation et de la pensée critique dans un monde en transformation rapide.

2025 Essai Brouillon

Résumé : Dans un monde marqué par des transformations rapides, la connaissance et la pensée critique sont plus importantes que jamais. Cet essai examine le rôle de l'éducation et du savoir dans la construction de sociétés résilientes.

Thèse : La connaissance est un pouvoir, mais c'est aussi une responsabilité. Dans une société en mutation, la capacité à penser de manière critique, à remettre en question les certitudes et à apprendre tout au long de la vie est essentielle.
